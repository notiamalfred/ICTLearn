Analog vs Digital Data

Two ways to represent data

TypeDescriptionExamples
Analog data Represented in a physical way with continuous values, such as sound waves, written text and drawings. Photographic films, cassette tape.
Digital data Represented in discrete symbols (like "0" and "1"), such as text documents, digital photos and audio files. Usually stored and used in devices like computers and smartphones. Memory cards in digital cameras, optical discs.

Common audio formats: MP3 (compressed audio) and WAV (raw audio).

Digitalisation

Two parts: discretisation and quantisation

Digitalisation is the conversion of analog data to digital data.

Discretisation (sampling)

Take an analog signal as an example. An analog signal can take on an uncountable set of continuous values. Its quantity varies from time to time.

To convert an analog signal into a digital signal, the value of the signal should be sampled at a regular time interval. The length of this time interval determines the sampling rate. This process is called discretisation.

Quantisation

After discretisation, sampled values are rounded to a fixed set of discrete values, such as integers.

The analog signal is stored as a sequence of integers. This sequence is digital data, which is usually represented by binary numbers in digital storage. This process is called quantisation.

Key relationship: A higher sampling rate means more samples are taken in a given period of time. The resulting data is thus closer to the original waveform and has less data loss during digitalisation.

Digitalisation of Sounds and Videos

From sound source to storage

Sound waves are analog data that can be converted into digital data through the following process:

Sound source → Microphone → Sound card → Data storage (analog signal) (analog signal) (binary number)

The sound card converts the analog electrical signal into binary numbers (digital signal). Audio editing software can then be used to edit the digital audio.

For playback, the digital signal is converted back into an analog electrical signal, sent to a speaker, and played as sound.

Digitalisation of Text and Images

From book to electronic book

Converting physical books to electronic books is usually a digitalisation of text and images.

The process:

  1. A book is scanned by a scanner or a scanning application.
  2. The scanner outputs a digital image.
  3. The digital image is converted via optical character recognition (OCR) into editable text.
  4. The text is edited by a word processor to produce an electronic book.
  5. The electronic book can be printed as a physical book again.

File Size Calculation

Audio file size formula

For uncompressed audio:

File size (bits) = Channels × Duration (s) × Sample rate (Hz) × Bit depth (bits)

Worked example

A stereo audio file has the following properties:

PropertyValue
Duration3.6 s
Sample rate44100 Hz
Bit depth16 bit
Channels2
2 × 3.6 × 44100 × 16 = 5 080 320 bits ≈ 620 KB
